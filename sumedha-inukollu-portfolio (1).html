The Threshold Effect

Every dot below is a gene from a simplified differential-expression run. Move the two lines — fold-change and significance — and watch genes cross from "noise" into "signal," and back again. This is the exact decision I sat with while building biomarker lists for my thesis.

In my thesis on lymphoma, leukemia, and glioma transcriptomes, this exact line — where to call a gene "significant" — decided which genes we carried forward as candidate biomarkers. I don't take that line lightly, and I don't think anyone building diagnostics should either.

Why

The point of all this.

Personalized medicine already works — for the well-insured and the well-sequenced. I want to help build the version that works for everyone else, too.

I think about the person at the end of every pipeline I write — not as an abstraction, but as someone who deserves a diagnosis that doesn't depend on their zip code or income. That's the thread connecting a threshold slider on a volcano plot to a mutation-burden analysis in pediatric leukemia to whatever lab or team I eventually lead: get the details right, because the details are where the person is.
